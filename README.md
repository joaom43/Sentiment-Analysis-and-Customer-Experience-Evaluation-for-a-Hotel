# Sentiment-Analysis-and-Customer-Experience-Evaluation-for-a-Hotel

This repository presents a comprehensive sentiment analysis and data science project focused on evaluating customer experience and brand perception for Hotel Portinari. This work was conducted for the Data Mining II (ECDII) course as part of the MADSAD 2024/2025 program.

Our approach combines exploratory data analysis (EDA) with advanced natural language processing (NLP) and machine learning to extract actionable insights from guest reviews. The initial exploration analyzed rating distributions across various factors, revealing that positive reviews are generally brief, averaging 30 to 35 characters, while negative reviews are significantly longer and expand as the rating score drops. To delve into the emotional tone of the feedback, we applied several state-of-the-art sentiment classification models. While an initial VADER model provided a basic positive, neutral, and negative baseline, we utilized more nuanced models like RoBERTa-base-go-emotions (identifying up to 27 emotions) and j-hartmann/emotion-english-roberta-large to capture a detailed spectrum of human emotions.  By employing the bert-base-uncased model for topic modeling, we discovered that guest feedback primarily centers around a single core topic containing five main aspects: the room, breakfast, the hotel in general, location, and the bathroom. We subsequently applied aspect-based sentiment analysis (ABSA) to these specific categories. The results demonstrated that the hotel's breakfast and location are highly regarded, frequently generating feelings of admiration. Conversely, the rooms, bathrooms, and the hotel overall require critical attention, as they consistently trigger sentiments of disappointment among guests.  

The analysis also includes user clustering and temporal evaluations spanning from 2022 to 2025. The data indicates that solo travelers tend to leave the most positive reviews, whereas couples and families report more negative experiences, particularly concerning the bathroom and overall hotel facilities. Furthermore, we uncovered a strong correlation between guest satisfaction and the length of the stay; guests remaining for eight or more nights submit substantially more positive feedback compared to those booking single-night stays. All corresponding charts, data distributions, and deeper insights are thoroughly documented in GW2_Group4.pdf to provide a data-driven framework that supports strategic decision-making in the hospitality sector.
